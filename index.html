finance

Finance tooling built for work that's already part of the job. Python, synthetic data, readable logic — every project ships a validation step that must pass before its dashboard publishes, and several of them caught real errors in my own models before you could.

Physical AI & robotics

Where a deployed unit is a capital asset, acquisition runs through pilots rather than trials, and the dominant cost is a training run whose accounting treatment is still contested.

Robot Fleet Unit Economics →

A per-robot P&L across 181 deployed units: utilization vs uptime, contribution against the depreciation each unit must out-earn, and payback per unit. 18% of the fleet never repays its hardware — for two different reasons that need opposite fixes.

● Live dashboard

Training Run Capitalization →

Is a training run R&D expense or an intangible asset? Both treatments modelled side by side, with the identity that keeps them honest: over a run's full life they charge the same total. I predicted the EBITDA gap would compress; validation proved it compounds.

● Live dashboard

Pilot-to-Production Funnel →

True CAC counts the pilots you lost — $580K, 5.3× the naive figure. Conversion collapses past nine months, making pilot purgatory datable. Run only qualified pilots and CAC falls to 31% of three-year TCV.

SaaS

Thirty metrics in five sections — growth, customer health, efficiency, financial, AI-specific — every one with its definition on the page. LTV:CAC is flagged as formulaic right on the scorecard.

Payments

Reconciliation at transaction scale, and contribution measured in basis points per transaction rather than margin per customer.

Payments Reconciliation Engine →

50,000 transactions matched against a bank settlement file. Every break classified — timing, amount, missing, duplicate, FX, fee — aged by exposure and routed to an owner. All 1,148 seeded breaks found and correctly labeled.

Payments Unit Economics →

Contribution per transaction across 120,000 transactions by rail, region, and merchant segment. Contribution waterfall, cross-border premium, and mix-shift sensitivity.

Core finance — any industry

The work that doesn't change with the business model: cash, statements, scenarios, variance, controls.

Integrated Three-Statement Model →

Income statement, balance sheet, and indirect cash flow, line by line across twelve quarters with scenario tabs. A = L + E to the cent in every column, or the model refuses to publish.

● Live dashboard

Monte Carlo Scenario Planner →

10,000 simulations of 12 quarters. P10/P50/P90 fans for ARR and cash, floor-breach probability, and a tornado ranking which assumption actually drives the outcome.

● Live dashboard

13-Week Cash Forecast →

Direct-method forecast from the AR aging, the AP aging, and the recurring commitments in neither. Low point, covenant floor test, and collections stress scenarios.

● Live dashboard

Cash Burn & Runway →

Gross vs net burn, runway trajectory, and the fundraise trigger drawn as a date on the chart. Runway is measured on operating burn — a trailing average can't absorb a one-time prepay without printing a cliff that isn't real.

● Live dashboard

Continuous Controls Monitor →

Seven audit detectors over a year of AP and T&E — duplicates, split invoices, Benford's law, policy and velocity anomalies. 100% recall against seeded ground truth, false-positive rate reported.

● Live dashboard

Variance Narrator →

Budget-vs-actuals in, board-ready variance commentary out. Python computes every figure; Claude drafts the narrative. The model never does arithmetic and never sees a number it wasn't handed.
