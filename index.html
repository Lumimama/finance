finance

Finance decision tools, built with AI — for founders, finance teams, executives, and boards, from early-stage startups to global technology companies. Working examples of one repeatable motion: identify the operating question, define the finance logic, build the tool, translate the result into a decision. These are portfolio demonstrations, not one recommended suite for a single company — each card states the stage it fits, who runs it, and the decision it answers. Every project ships a validation step that must pass before its dashboard publishes.

Business-model-specific tools

Different business models break generic finance tooling in different places. These are built for the economics that make each model unusual — hardware as a capital asset, inference as COGS, revenue in basis points.

Robotics & physical AI

Robot Fleet Unit Economics →

A per-robot P&L across 181 deployed units: utilization vs uptime, contribution against the depreciation each unit must out-earn, and payback per unit. 18% of the fleet never repays its hardware — for two different reasons that need opposite fixes.

  • Best for Robotics and hardware-deployment companies, Series A+
  • Primary user Founder, finance, and operations
  • Decision Which units will never repay their hardware — and for each, redeploy, renegotiate, or wait out the contract?
● Live dashboard

Training Run Capitalization →

Is a training run R&D expense or an intangible asset? Both treatments modelled side by side, with the identity that keeps them honest: over a run's full life they charge the same total. I predicted the EBITDA gap would compress; validation proved it compounds.

  • Best for AI and robotics companies with large training spend
  • Primary user CFO, auditors, and board
  • Decision Expense or capitalize the training run — and what does each treatment do to EBITDA credibility?
● Live dashboard

Pilot-to-Production Funnel →

True CAC counts the pilots you lost — $580K, 5.3× the naive figure. Conversion collapses past nine months, making pilot purgatory datable. Run only qualified pilots and CAC falls to 31% of three-year TCV.

  • Best for Hardware and enterprise-AI companies that sell through pilots
  • Primary user Founder, finance, and sales
  • Decision What does a production customer truly cost including lost pilots, and which pilots should never start?
● Live dashboard

  • Best for AI companies choosing a monetization model
  • Primary user Founder and pricing team
  • Decision Subscription, metered, royalty, or hybrid — and separately, is OEM reach worth the concentration it creates?

Accounting, controls & close

The reliability layer — revenue recognized correctly, spend monitored continuously, consolidations that tie, commentary generated from computed figures. The part of finance that makes the charts trustworthy.

Technical accounting

Multi-Entity Consolidation — ASC 830 →

US parent + three foreign subs: translation vs remeasurement, with CTA computed two independent ways every month — a plug always balances, which is exactly why it can't be the only computation. The intercompany matrix catches a seeded ¥27,000 break and flags it as a transposition: the difference divides by 9.

  • Best for Any company with foreign subsidiaries, Series B+
  • Primary user Controller and auditors
  • Decision Do the eliminations net, does CTA prove itself, and is the equity move translation or performance?
● Live dashboard

Lease Accounting — ASC 842 →

A 26-lease register where every schedule must amortize to exactly zero and the footnote must tie to the liability. The largest lease never uses the word: a $10.1M "GPU capacity services agreement" with dedicated racks — the embedded-lease question every AI company's compute contracts are quietly asking.

  • Best for Any company with offices, fleets, or compute contracts — especially AI
  • Primary user Controller and auditors
  • Decision Which contracts are leases (whatever their title page says), and is each one classified defensibly?
● Live dashboard

Stock-Based Compensation — ASC 718 →

A grant ledger where the expense engine must match an independent closed-form recomputation at 96 month-ends. Straight-line vs graded side by side, a RIF that reverses in-cliff expense at actuals, and a repricing costed the way 718 demands: incremental fair value only — an underwater option still cost what it cost.

  • Best for Any venture-backed company; essential from Series B
  • Primary user Controller, comp committee, and auditors
  • Decision What does equity comp actually cost by period — and what did the repricing add, precisely?
● Live dashboard
