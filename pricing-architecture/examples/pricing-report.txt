WHAT EACH MODEL WINS AND LOSES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  highest revenue        royalty         $83.60M
  most forecastable      subscription    0.1% month-over-month volatility
  worst concentration    royalty         61% of revenue in top 3 accounts

  royalty produces the most revenue AND the worst concentration.
  Revenue is not the tiebreak; a business where 61% of revenue sits with three
  counterparties has handed them pricing power and its own roadmap.

  Royalty wins revenue only because it reaches 2.4x the units. That is not a pricing choice --
  it is a go-to-market bet: reach, paid for with the customer
  relationship. Compare it to the direct models on revenue alone and
  you have made a category error.

  Among DIRECT models, hybrid sits between the other two on both
  axes: $34.80M revenue, 3.4% volatility.

  LIMITATION: demand here is exogenous and identical across models,
  so this cannot value hybrid's expansion motion or the churn avoided
  by not overcharging light users -- the two reasons companies actually
  choose it. On the axes measurable here subscription wins among direct
  models, and that conclusion is weaker than it looks.
