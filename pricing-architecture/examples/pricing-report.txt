  model                revenue    exit ARR  gross margin  volatility  top-3 conc.
  subscription         $48.98M     $49.22M        95.7%        0.1%          32%

  royalty              $34.83M     $35.00M        93.5%        0.1%          32%
  hybrid               $34.80M     $34.87M        94.0%        3.4%          30%

  On identical units, subscription earns the most -- it simply charges
  the most per robot. Royalty priced on the SAME base earns
  $34.83M vs $48.98M;
  as a PRICE it is strictly worse, because the rate is lower.

QUESTION 2 -- DISTRIBUTION.  Royalty + OEM reach (2.4x the units).
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  royalty + OEM reach     $83.60M     $84.01M        97.3%        0.1%          61%

  Royalty wins revenue ONLY by changing distribution, not by
  changing price: $83.60M against $48.98M for subscription.
  And the concentration -- 61% of revenue in three
  counterparties, against 32% when the same
  rate is billed direct -- comes from the DISTRIBUTION choice, not
  the pricing one. That is the trade: reach, paid for by handing
  three partners the customer relationship and the renewal.

  Among the four PRICES, hybrid sits between subscription and
  metering on both axes: $34.80M revenue, 3.4% volatility.

  LIMITATION: demand is exogenous, so this cannot value hybrid's
  expansion motion or the churn avoided by not overcharging light
  users -- the two reasons companies actually choose it. On the axes
  measured here subscription wins, and that is weaker than it looks.
